Adafruit Micro-Lipo Charger with USB Type C Jack

Why: the first charger among edakit's test circuits – no reference circuit has one (power is three AA cells beside USB), and no other open design charges a cell. It puts a linear Li-ion charger with its behaviour model on a published board: the current R_PROG programs, held against the MCP73831's 500 mA and the cell's 1C (LIM.i/charge, LIM.backfeed into the cell), the charger's heat at a low cell (LIM.t), a second charge current set by a solder jumper, a LiPo pack whose JST plug can come reversed (PATH.reverse on a battery holder), two status LEDs on a tri-state output – the domain power with a real charger, and HINT.lithium and HINT.charge_termination on a part makers use. Makers build and copy it everywhere: the MCP73831 with a PROG resistor, two LEDs and a JST PH socket is the charger of most hobby LiPo projects – Adafruit's Micro-Lipo boards, the same circuit on Feathers and countless clones –, so a false alarm here would meet many.

Adafruit Industries (Limor Fried/Ladyada; the schematic's AUTHOR attribute K. Townsend), Adafruit-MicroLipo-PCB, https://github.com/adafruit/Adafruit-MicroLipo-PCB at commit d8ab4b0 (d8ab4b0f00c69a9f337a8d697bd15e0b9bae2e25, 2022-11-10), the Eagle schematic Adafruit USB C microlipo charger.sch (Eagle 9.5.0 XML, last changed in commit b06c550 of 2019-11-05) – the board Adafruit sells as product 4410, "Adafruit Micro-Lipo Charger for LiPoly Batt with USB Type C Jack" (https://www.adafruit.com/product/4410) –, read on 2026-11-03 from a read-only clone. The netlist was resolved from the schematic's XML (parts, nets, each device's pin-to-pad connects) by a script outside this repository and checked against the board file Adafruit USB C microlipo charger.brd of the same commit: all 44 pads of the 15 connected parts are on the same signals there. The product page was read the same day for what the schematic does not say (the jumper's purpose, the cells it is meant for). · SVG

simLIM.tU1realIn the 500 mA mode the SOT-23-5 dissipates 0.57 W from a cell at 3.7 V and 1.1 W from one at 3.0 V: 157–278 °C at 230 K/W. The MCP73831 then regulates its temperature and cuts the current (DS20001984H 6.1.1.3: 1.54 W worst case) – the 500 mA the jumper promises are not reached from a low cell; edakit's model has no thermal regulation and shows the dissipation.
simSIM.stateLED1known gapThe charger model does not drive STAT (the status outputs are among what the simulation leaves out, decision 17 of rules-at-scale.md: edakit check --coverage lists them): STAT floats in the model, and the two LEDs carry the current of the chain 5V – LED1 – R1 – R2 – LED2 – GND, 2 µA to 1.6 mA by the corner – the red LED too dim while charging, too bright with the cell full. On the board STAT is low while charging and lights it with about 6 mA through 470 Ω.
simSIM.stateLED2known gapAs LED1: with STAT not driven by the model, the green LED carries the chain's current while the cell charges (up to 1.6 mA, expected dark) and too little with the cell full (0.7 mA, expected on) – on the board STAT high lights it with a few mA through 470 Ω.

edakit's false alarms on this design, fixed

  • A charger's status LEDs, each behind its series resistor on the tri-state STAT, taken for a floating net (LVL.undefined/open_drain on STAT) – an LED cathode straight on an output counted as its load, one behind a resistor did not.
  • R4 behind the solder jumper SJ1 – the 500 mA mode – counted by neither LIM.i/charge nor the simulation's charger model, which read only resistors straight from PROG to ground: the check now holds the position that programs the most, the model the contacts a load closes.
