PATH paths

What a reversed, unfed or misplaced supply reaches – 3 codes.

Each engine decides one kind of relation; a finding's code is ENGINE.relation. Severities are the catalog's defaults – a project's domains may set others; a grade is a sub-case with its own severity, a topic the part of a code a domain switches on.

PATH.reverse reverse polarity blocked

errorpolarized: warning

What a reversed source (a battery holder, a source marked reversible) reaches through fuses, switches, push buttons and connectors is only capacitors without polarity, holders, the anode of a blocking diode – on a negative rail (the protected net declared at or below 0 V) its cathode – or the drain of a PMOS whose source and gate are outside; a polarized capacitor it reaches is reverse biased (grade polarized).

  • reverse polarity from the topology: what a reversed source (battery holders, "source": "reversible" on the component) reaches through fuses, switches, push buttons and connectors may only be a capacitor, the anode of a diode whose cathode is outside, or the drain of a PMOS whose source and gate are outside – finds e.g.: the protection diode bypassed, an IC directly on the battery
Why
A battery inserted the wrong way drives the supply negative; every IC on that rail conducts through its substrate diodes and dies within milliseconds.
What to do
A Schottky diode in series, or a PMOS in the supply path with its gate to ground (block reverse-pmos).
Sources
  1. TI SLVA139, Reverse Current/Battery Protection Circuits (2003) – https://www.ti.com/lit/an/slva139/slva139.pdf

PATH.unfed every supply pin reached by a supply

error

Every supply pin (power_in) is on a net a supply reaches – a power output, a battery holder, a connector with a vbus pin or marked as a source, a simulation source – directly or through fuses, switches, push buttons, connectors, inductors, MOSFETs, resistors up to 100 Ω and diodes in forward direction – on a negative rail from cathode to anode.

  • ground and supply pins: every ground pin on the ground net (or one joined to it by a 0 Ω bridge or an inductor, or in grounds); every power_in pin on a net that a supply reaches – a power_output pin, a battery holder, a connector with a vbus pin or in "source": "reversible" on the component, a simulation source – directly or through fuses, switches, push buttons, connectors, inductors, MOSFETs, resistors up to 100 Ω and diodes in forward direction – finds e.g.: a rail split by a typo in its name, a ground pin on a net of its own, VDD swapped with a signal
Why
A rail split by a typo in a net name kills every board and passes every other check.
What to do
Put the pin on its rail; usually a typo in a net name or two pins swapped.
Sources
  1. Lesson of the reference project (pytree MVP risk review, item 10, 2026-10-23): pin-to-net checked by hand for every IC, about 20 minutes, and nothing repeats it after the next change
  2. edakit handbook 4.1 (pin types ground, power_in, power_output) and 3.3 (0 Ω bridges)

PATH.ground ground pins on ground

error

Every ground pin is on the ground net (or one joined to it by an inductor, or in the graph's grounds), and no supply pin is on ground.

Why
A ground pin on a net of its own leaves the part unpowered; a supply pin on ground shorts the rail.
What to do
Put the pin on ground; a ground separate on purpose goes into the graph's grounds.
Sources
  1. Lesson of the reference project (pytree MVP risk review, item 10, 2026-10-23): pin-to-net checked by hand for every IC, about 20 minutes, and nothing repeats it after the next change
  2. edakit handbook 4.1 (pin types ground, power_in, power_output) and 3.3 (0 Ω bridges)
