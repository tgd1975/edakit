error

LIM LIMITS – every quantity within its limit

LIM.v voltage within its limit

errorpolarized: warninginject: warning

Every voltage a part sees stays within the limit of its entry: across a capacitor, reverse across a polarized capacitor (vr_max_v, grade polarized), an input above its own part's supply (vin_rel_max_v, grade inject), reverse across a diode or LED, VCE, VEB, VDS, VGS, on a pin (v_max_v; an active pin not below −0.3 V, a negative rail's supply pin – role vee – excepted), on a fuse, switch, resistor or connector, and every limit a transient run sets on a net. Stage G takes the declared net ranges times the derating factor (1.5 on capacitors and semiconductors), stage E the simulated value against the derated limit.

  • voltage domains: every net's range within v_max_v of every pin on it – finds e.g.: a 5-V signal on a 3.3-V GPIO
  • voltage ratings with margin: capacitors (×1.5), diodes VRRM (×1.5), BJT VCEO, MOSFET VDS/VGS (×1.5), fuses, switches, connectors – finds e.g.: a 6.3-V capacitor on a 5-V rail
  • stress of every part against its ratings with derating: resistor power ≤ 50 %, currents ≤ 80 %, capacitor/diode/transistor voltages ≤ 67 %, LED forward current and reverse voltage, fuse hold current at ambient, connector, switch and relay contact current, relay coil voltage, the current of every MCU output (gpio_i_max_a) and of all outputs together, sourced and sunk (gpio_i_total_max_a) – each pin may be fine and the sum beyond the package; the junction temperature of every part with rth_ja_k_per_w against its tj_max_c, and on a board of touch_c against the touch limit (5.3)
  • transient limits: in every run of transients each limit of its expect – a net's lowest voltage ≥ min_v, highest ≤ max_v, a source's peak current ≤ i_max_a – and every MCU's supply pin, once it has reached its vin_min_v (vsys_min_v), never below it again before the run ends; actual value with the time it occurs ("2.985 V at 1.3 ms")
Why
A part at its absolute maximum is at the edge of failure; ceramic capacitors lose capacitance and lifetime near their rated voltage, an input beyond its supply conducts through its protection diodes. The margin covers what a DC view does not see.
What to do
A part with a higher rating, level shifting at a pin, or a correct (less pessimistic) net range v.

  2. edakit handbook 3.3 (the margins of the reference project)
  3. edakit handbook 5.3

LIM.hazardous hazardous voltage recognised, not checked

error

Every net's declared voltage range stays within the ES1 limit of IEC 62368-1 (60 V DC, catalog/tables/iec62368.toml); a net beyond it is hazardous voltage, and the finding says that edakit checks neither its spacing nor its isolation. An AC net is judged by its peak against the DC limit while the AC limit is not read.

Why
edakit's checks are made for safety extra-low voltage. Above the ES1 limit a fault is a shock or a fire, the safeguards – creepage, clearance, insulation, the mains input – are the standard's and a test lab's, and edakit measures none of them; a board with such a net that passed every check would look checked where it was not. So edakit says it, once per net, as an error a project must accept on purpose (rules-at-scale.md, decision 7).
What to do
Keep the hazardous voltage off the board – a bought, certified supply that hands the board only safety extra-low voltage – or accept the finding on the net with its reason ("mains only on the bought relay module's terminals, enclosure IP40") and have the safeguards reviewed outside edakit (domain mains: the reviewers' questions).

LIM.i current within its limit

errormargin: warningcell: warningsupply_pin: warningcharge: warning

A USB receptacle that powers the board: consider what the port gives – 100 mA before the device is configured, 500 mA after (USB 2.0), 900 mA on USB 3.2 single lane, 1.5 A or 3 A only where a Type-C source advertises it on CC and the board reads the advertisement.

Why
A board that takes what its LEDs, motors or radio need from VBUS works at a phone charger and browns out or trips a laptop's port: the host gives five unit loads only after enumeration, and 1.5 A or 3 A only to a sink that reads the Rp advertisement on CC (maker-coverage PWR 25, LED 7, LED 8, MCU 35). Rd alone on CC says the board is a sink, not how much it may draw.
What to do
Keep the board's draw within 500 mA (100 mA before configuration) – count it with edakit sim against the source's i_max_a –, or read the advertisement (a CC comparator or a PD controller) and limit the load to it, or supply the heavy load from its own input.
Sources

  2. USB Type-C Cable and Connector Specification Release 2.0 (August 2019), 2.3.4 and Table 2-1: Default (500 mA USB 2.0, 900 mA or 1,500 mA USB 3.2), USB Type-C Current @ 1.5 A and @ 3.0 A – https://www.usb.org/document-library/usb-type-cr-cable-and-connector-specification-release-20

HINT.usb_inrush more than 10 µF on USB VBUS

note

A USB receptacle whose VBUS net carries more than 10 µF directly (the capacitors' value_f): consider a soft start or less capacitance ahead of it – USB 2.0 allows a device 10 µF at attach; HINT.bulk asks for a bulk capacitor at the same input, and the two meet here.

Why
At attach the device's capacitance charges from the hub's VBUS; above 10 µF in parallel with 44 Ω the hub's VBUS droops more than 330 mV and the other ports' devices may reset (USB 2.0, 7.2.4.1). A bulk capacitor at a power input is good advice for a supply cable (HINT.bulk) and too much for a USB port.
What to do
Keep at most 10 µF on VBUS and put the bulk capacitance behind a load switch with soft start (or a regulator that limits its inrush), or accept the hint with the inrush measured or the port known (a charger, not a hub).

HINT.cable_line a signal line on a cable

note

A signal net runs through a cable: consider what the cable's length and the world at its end do to it.

Why
A long lead picks up interference and has capacitance: an input needs a stronger pull and filtering, an output's edges ring, a clock or data line needs its return beside it (maker-coverage MOT 31, AUD 28–30, LED 4).
What to do
Inputs: a pull-up of 10 kΩ or less, an RC with a Schmitt-trigger input, a TVS; outputs: a series resistor at the source; data and clocks: a return wire beside each signal, twisted pairs.
